Welcome to SceneFlow!

WHAT IS SCENEFLOW?
SceneFlow is a script-to-screen synchronization tool. It is designed to help you visualize, structure, and sync your AI video prompts with actual video timelines. Whether you write in traditional screenplay format or technical state-driven scripts, SceneFlow acts as the bridge between written instructions and the generated frame.

TWO WAYS TO WRITE
SceneFlow supports two distinct formatting workflows:
1. Screenplay Format: A traditional, human-readable script layout (sluglines, action lines, character dialogue, and parentheticals).
2. Auteur Script Format: A compact, technical state-machine layout wrapped in brief blocks, using chained arrows and bold tags for precision generation.

[A Quick Note on Style Evolution]
(Screenplay vs. Prompting Input)
Since March 2026, my primary workflow has shifted away from traditional screenplays in favor of the precision Auteur Script style, as my core goal is providing direct, state-driven prompt inputs for video generation models. However, you can always write in traditional screenplay format as raw text depending on your creative goal. SceneFlow is built to support both workflows seamlessly.

Both styles share the same powerful Staging, Viewport, and Timeline Cue systems.

I. THE STAGING BLOCK:
(Setting global foundation & guardrails)

Staging is the foundation of your scene. Use the Staging Block to define rules that don't change from beat to beat—such as lighting setup, master wardrobe, spatial logic, or core intent.

To create a staging section, wrap your metadata in double brackets. SceneFlow automatically conceals the raw text and replaces it with an interactive "STAGING" badge. Click the badge anytime to inspect your scene parameters.

[[STAGING]]
[[INTENT]]
Define the high-level identity, subject, and core emotional beat of the scene.
[[/INTENT]]
[[LOGIC]]
List hard guardrails for the model: spatial continuity, 180-degree axis rules, and hand/prop permanence.
[[/LOGIC]]
[[AESTHETIC]]
Medium: 35mm film texture.
Palette: Deep navy, warm amber tungsten.
Lighting: Directional key light from stage left.
[[/AESTHETIC]]
[[OPENING]]
Describe the locked first frame (S0): subject position, camera framing, and active props before action begins.
[[/OPENING]]
[[/STAGING]]

II. SCREENPLAY FORMATTING:
(Traditional human-readable layout)

[Sluglines / Scene Headings]
(Defining time and place)
Sluglines establish the physical environment. Start a line with INT. (Interior) or EXT. (Exterior). SceneFlow renders these as highlighted scene banners.

INT. SCENEFLOW DESIGN SUITE - DAY

[Action Lines]
(Describing physical movement & framing)
Any regular text describes what appears on screen. Use uppercase lines for bold visual emphasis.

The cursor blinks on a clean slate. A CREATOR (30s) leans in, their eyes reflecting the glow of infinite creative control.

[Dialogue Formatting]
(Assigning spoken lines to characters)
To format dialogue, type the character name in ALL CAPS followed by a colon. SceneFlow automatically centers the character name and their speech.

CREATOR:
Everything starts with intent.

[Parentheticals]
(Directing tone and performance)
Wrap text in parentheses immediately under a character name to give performance directions. These appear italicized and centered.

CREATOR:
(whispering with excitement)
It's not just a script; it's a flow.

[Technical Notes & Effects]
(SFX, VFX, and camera notes)
Use technical cues for sound, visual effects, and shot notes:
- Start a line with SFX: or VFX: for dedicated italic effect callouts.
- Wrap bracketed text like [CLOSE-UP] for technical camera notes.

SFX: The soft, rhythmic hum of a workstation.
[CLOSE-UP – MONITOR DISPLAY]

[Structure Helpers]
(Dividers and part titles)
To organize larger scripts, use horizontal separators, part tags, or Roman numeral headers:
- Use three dashes (---) for a full-width line break.
- Use PART 1 or PART 2 for scene division headers.
- Use Roman numerals (e.g., I. THE BEGINNING) for formal chapter titles.

III. AUTEUR SCRIPT FORMATTING:
(Technical state-machine chaining)

[Auteur Script Style]
(Precision prompt engineering)
For high-precision models, wrap your timeline execution in [<BRIEF>] tags. SceneFlow renders this in a dedicated monospace card and applies two automatic layout engines:

- Waterfall Indentation: Every "->" delimiter automatically creates a new line with nested indentation, turning a long prompt into a clean visual beat chain.
- Highlighted Anchors: Any word inside brackets like [CAM], [ACT], [AUDIO], or [STATE OUT] is automatically bolded for quick scanning.

[<BRIEF>]
[CAM 01] MS, eye-level lockoff -> [ACT] Creator types the first command -> Creator: "Let's build." -> <Mechanical keyboard click> -> [STATE OUT] Frame locked in clean focus
[</BRIEF>]

IV. WORKING WITH CUES:
(Syncing script text to the video timeline)

[Syncing Text to Time]
(The core superpower of SceneFlow)
The real power of SceneFlow is linking your script segments directly to specific moments in the video timeline via 'Cues'.

[8 Dedicated Color Categories]
(Theme-calibrated visual tagging)
SceneFlow provides 8 calibrated color categories:
- Dialogue (Yellow/Amber)
- Action (Blue/Sky)
- Camera (Green/Emerald)
- Shot (Purple)
- Audio / SFX (Orange)
- VFX (Cyan/Teal)
- Transition (Pink)
- Environment (Slate/Grey)

[How to Add a Cue]
(Creating timeline highlights)
Switch to 'Edit' mode and highlight any text segment in your script. The 'Cue Settings' modal pops up instantly, allowing you to pick a category and set Start and End timestamps.

[Editing, Overlaps & Deleting]
(Managing existing cues)
Click any highlighted cue in the script to adjust its duration or category. If multiple cues overlap, click to pick the exact cue you want to inspect. To remove a cue, click the Trash icon in the left 'Timeline Cues' panel.

[Alignment & Alternatives]
(Smart text tracking)
If you edit script text after creating cues, click 'Align' in the sidebar to automatically re-anchor highlights. If a repeated phrase matches the wrong instance, click 'Find Alternative' to cycle occurrences.

V. PLAYBACK & VIEWPORT CONTROLS:
(Optimizing your review workspace)

[Auto-Scroll & Focus Mode]
(Following the action in real-time)
In 'Playback' mode, the script highlights and scrolls in real-time with the video. Click the 'Focus Mode' dropdown next to Auto-Scroll to filter which cue categories trigger scrolling (e.g., follow Dialogue only).

[Focus Line Position]
(Customizing the viewport anchor)
Choose where the active cue centers in your viewport during playback: Top (35%), Center (50%), or Bottom (65%).

[Script Width Presets]
(Tailoring the reading canvas)
Select from 5 width presets (from Compact to Full) to adjust your reading canvas for side-by-side video inspection.
